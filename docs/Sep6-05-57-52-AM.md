# Gesture training explanation

Completed: 2026-09-06 05:57:52 EDT (America/New_York).

Request: Explain how gesture training works after the user reported poor MediaPipe recognition on the iPhone.

No app code, model weights, camera recordings, or deployment changed. Current phones use Google's pretrained model; no custom training or online learning has occurred. The iPhone also runs handwritten directional and three-finger rules before canned-label fallback, so these can override the learned classifier. Their fixed 0.8 acceptance score is not calibrated confidence. Raw landmarks, raw model labels, rule overrides, and stabilized output need to be examined separately before attributing poor performance to training data.

MediaPipe's pipeline detects a hand, estimates 21 landmarks, and classifies hand geometry through a gesture embedding/classification pipeline. Customization normally trains a lightweight gesture classifier while retaining the pretrained hand detector/landmarker. Google's Model Maker workflow loads labeled image folders (including a none class), extracts landmarks, trains and evaluates the classifier, and exports an on-device gesture_recognizer.task bundle. Images without detected hands are omitted from its training data; this customization will not repair camera rotation or unreliable landmark tracking.

Suggested project workflow: record labeled examples from the actual front cameras across people, hands, lighting, distances, angles, and natural transitions. For the requested three commands, classify fist, index-point, and none; derive up/down from the original image-space pointing vector. Include both pointing directions during collection and reject near-horizontal directions. Keep full recording sessions/people separated for validation and testing, rather than distributing neighboring video frames across splits. Measure false commands, missed gestures, and time to activation in addition to accuracy. Train offline on a computer and bundle the exported model in the app; keep temporal command handling separate.

The existing PC scripts collect landmark features and fit a standardized k-nearest-neighbor classifier saved as custom_gestures.npz. This is a separate training path from MediaPipe Model Maker, and that model is not currently installed in the iPhone app.
